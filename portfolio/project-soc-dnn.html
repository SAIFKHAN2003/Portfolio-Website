Problem Statement

Why Accurate SoC Matters

State-of-Charge is the fundamental metric that determines EV driving range, charging strategy, and battery health. Conventional Coulomb counting methods accumulate error over time and are sensitive to temperature variation. Model-based observers (Kalman filter, EKF) require precise equivalent circuit parameters that drift with aging.

Coulomb counting error compounds to ±15% over a full cycle
EKF requires accurate battery model parameters — impractical for aging cells
Temperature effects introduce ±8% SoC deviation in conventional methods
Deep learning can learn implicit relationships without explicit cell modeling

Model Architecture

Deep Neural Network Design

A feedforward DNN with 4 hidden layers was designed using TensorFlow/Keras. Input features: terminal voltage, current, temperature, and elapsed time. Output: instantaneous SoC (0–100%). Trained on battery cycling dataset with WLTP and UDDS drive profiles.

Input layer: 4 features (V, I, T, t)
Hidden layers: [128, 64, 32, 16] neurons, ReLU activation
Output: single SoC value, sigmoid activation
Optimizer: Adam, LR = 0.001 with decay schedule

Methodology

Dataset & Training Pipeline

Battery cycling dataset from public Li-ion characterization database (NASA and CALCE). Preprocessing: outlier removal, Z-score normalization, sliding window segmentation. 80/10/10 train/validation/test split. Evaluated against Coulomb counting and EKF baselines on held-out test cycles.

Dataset: 50,000+ timestep samples across charge/discharge cycles
Feature engineering: delta voltage, dV/dt computed as additional inputs
Dropout (p=0.2) used for regularization to prevent overfitting
Early stopping with patience=10 on validation MAE

Results & Outcomes

Model Performance

The DNN significantly outperformed both Coulomb counting and extended Kalman filter baselines across all test conditions, with particularly strong performance in dynamic drive cycles and elevated temperature scenarios.

MAE: 1.34% — vs. Coulomb counting at 4.8%
RMSE: 1.82% across full UDDS test cycle
Robust to ±15°C temperature variation
Inference time: <2ms — suitable for BMS deployment

Learnings & Future Work

Key Takeaways

This research demonstrated that data-driven SoC estimation can substantially outperform conventional methods, particularly under dynamic conditions and aging. The work was accepted and presented at the IC3ECSBHI-2025 international conference in 2025.

LSTM/GRU recurrent models may capture temporal dependencies better than feedforward DNN
Transfer learning could enable model adaptation to new cell chemistries with minimal data
Embedded deployment (STM32 + TFLite) would validate real-time BMS integration
SoH (State-of-Health) estimation is the natural extension of this work

Research Timeline

From idea to publication

Phase 1 — Ideation
Problem Formulation & Literature Survey
Aug 2024 — Sep 2024

Surveyed 40+ papers on battery SoC estimation methods: Coulomb counting, OCV lookup, Kalman filter variants, and data-driven approaches. Identified DNN as underexplored in embedded BMS context. Defined research gap and formulated approach.

Literature Survey Research Gap Problem Formulation
Phase 2 — Data
Dataset Collection & Preprocessing
Sep 2024 — Oct 2024

Downloaded and cleaned NASA and CALCE Li-ion battery cycling datasets. Applied Z-score normalization, handled missing timesteps, computed derivative features (dV/dt, dI/dt). Split into 80/10/10 train/val/test sets, ensuring temporal integrity of cycling sequences.

Python Pandas Data Cleaning Feature Engineering
Phase 3 — Development
DNN Architecture Design & Training
Oct 2024 — Nov 2024

Designed and iteratively optimized DNN architecture in TensorFlow/Keras. Performed hyperparameter tuning on layer depth, width, dropout rate, and learning rate schedule. Implemented early stopping and model checkpointing. Final model: 4 layers, 128-64-32-16 neurons.

TensorFlow Keras Hyperparameter Tuning Dropout
Phase 4 — Evaluation
Benchmarking & Results Analysis
Nov 2024 — Dec 2024

Benchmarked DNN against Coulomb counting and EKF implementations. Evaluated on held-out UDDS and WLTP drive cycles at 25°C and 40°C. Generated MAE, RMSE, and Max-error metrics. Analyzed error distribution across SoC range — model performed best between 20–80% SoC.

Benchmarking Error Analysis UDDS / WLTP
Phase 5 — Publication
Paper Writing & Conference Presentation
Dec 2024 — Feb 2025

Wrote and submitted paper to IC3ECSBHI-2025 International Conference. Received acceptance. Prepared presentation slides and delivered the paper to conference audience. Paper documents methodology, results, and future directions for embedded SoC estimation.
