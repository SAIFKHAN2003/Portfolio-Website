Problem Statement

India's Urban Solar Paradox

Urban apartment residents in India lack access to rooftop solar due to shared ownership structures. Centralized grid import dominates despite abundant rooftop space — residents pay full retail tariffs while surplus solar from individual consumers is underutilised. Regulatory frameworks are catching up but technical architecture remains undefined.

80% of urban electricity consumers are apartment dwellers — no direct rooftop access
Net metering penetration in India <0.5% of eligible connections (2023)
DISCOM buy-back tariffs are 40–60% below retail — disincentivises prosumers
P2P trading can unlock value for both prosumers and energy-poor consumers
02 / 06
System Design

P2P Microgrid Architecture

A residential cluster of 20 units shares a community solar array. Smart meters at each unit enable real-time energy accounting. Surplus energy from prosumers is credited to neighbours via a blockchain-inspired token ledger, with DISCOM as the settlement backstop.

Community solar: 50 kWp shared rooftop array
Smart meters with 15-min interval data logging
LV distribution: 415V, 3-phase, radial topology
Net metering + P2P token settlement mechanism

Methodology

PVsyst Modelling & Regulatory Analysis

PVsyst was used to simulate the 50 kWp community array using TMY (Typical Meteorological Year) data for New Delhi. Load profiles were synthesized from residential consumption surveys. Inverter sizing, cable sizing, and protection coordination were performed per IS/IEC standards. DISCOM net metering compliance was assessed against Delhi Electricity Regulatory Commission (DERC) guidelines.

PVsyst simulation: monthly yield, PR, and specific production analysis
Load diversity factor applied for coincident peak demand estimation
String inverter sizing: 5 × 10 kW units with MPPT optimization
Protection: RCCB + SPD at array level, OCR at community feeder

Results & Outcomes

Economic & Technical Performance

The proposed P2P microgrid showed compelling economics for both prosumers and consumers. Prosumers earn 20% more than DISCOM buy-back; consumers pay 18% less than grid retail. Simple payback period under 5 years with current CAPEX.

Annual generation: ~72,000 kWh — covers 85% of cluster demand
Performance ratio (PR): 78.4% — above industry benchmark
Payback period: 4.8 years at ₹45/Wp installed cost
CO₂ avoided: ~58 tonnes/year (grid emission factor 0.82 kgCO₂/kWh)

Project Timeline

From concept to Ideathon win

Phase 1 — Concept
Problem Identification & Regulatory Research
Jul 2024 — Aug 2024

Identified urban solar access gap as a key policy and engineering problem. Studied DERC net metering regulations, DISCOM interconnection procedures, and Ministry of New and Renewable Energy (MNRE) rooftop solar guidelines. Framed P2P energy trading model to exploit regulatory white space.

Regulatory Analysis DERC Guidelines MNRE Policy
Phase 2 — Technical Design
Load Profiling & System Sizing
Aug 2024 — Sep 2024

Conducted residential load survey across a sample of 20 apartments. Applied diversity factor and synthesized 15-min interval hourly load profiles. Sized the community solar array (50 kWp), inverters (5×10 kW), cabling (LV 415V radial), and protection coordination scheme per IS 13234 and IEC 60364.

Load Profiling PV Sizing Protection Design
Phase 3 — Simulation
PVsyst Yield Modelling
Sep 2024 — Oct 2024

Built detailed PVsyst model using New Delhi TMY meteorological data. Simulated monthly and annual energy yield, soiling losses, cable losses, and inverter efficiency curves. Optimised panel tilt (22°) and azimuth (south-facing) for maximum annual generation. PR of 78.4% achieved post all loss corrections.

PVsyst TMY Data Yield Simulation
Phase 4 — Economics
Techno-Economic Analysis & P2P Model
Oct 2024 — Nov 2024

Developed financial model: CAPEX at ₹45/Wp, OPEX at 1%/yr, 25-yr project life, 8% discount rate. Calculated NPV, IRR, and simple payback for prosumer and community perspectives. Designed P2P token pricing mechanism between DISCOM buy-back and retail tariff to incentivise both sides.

Financial Modelling NPV / IRR P2P Pricing
Phase 5 — Competition
DTU-Incubate IIF Ideathon 2024 — 🏆 Winner
Nov 2024

Presented the full concept to a panel of investors, faculty, and industry professionals at the DTU-Incubate IIF Ideathon. Competed against 60+ teams from Delhi NCR. Won first prize — jury highlighted the regulatory-aware design, quantified P2P economics, and clear deployment pathway as key differentiators.
